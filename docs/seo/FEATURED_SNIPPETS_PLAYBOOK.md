## 1. Paragraph Snippet Templates (Target: 42–55 Words)

### Query 1: "What is VAT in Nigeria?"
> **Target Format:** `<p class="featured-definition">` directly following an `<h2>` heading.  
> **Snippet Copy:**  
> *"Value Added Tax (VAT) in Nigeria is a consumption tax charged on the supply of taxable goods and services at a standard statutory rate of 7.5%, governed by the Value Added Tax Act and administered by the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS). All registered businesses must file and remit monthly VAT returns by the 21st day."* (55 words)

### Query 2: "What is a WHT Credit Note in Nigeria?"
> **Snippet Copy:**  
> *"A Withholding Tax (WHT) credit note in Nigeria is an official statutory receipt issued by the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) or State IRS certifying that tax was deducted at source by a client. Nigerian businesses record WHT credit notes as assets to legally offset their annual Company Income Tax liability."* (51 words)

### Query 3: "What is CAMA 2020 Section 374?"
> **Snippet Copy:**  
> *"Section 374 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020 mandates that every registered Nigerian company must preserve accounting records, journal vouchers, and financial statements for a minimum of six years from the date they are made. Failure to preserve statutory records constitutes an offence under Nigerian corporate law."* (50 words)

## 3. Ordered List Snippet Template

### Query: "How to reconcile a bank statement in Nigeria"
```markdown
1. Export your monthly PDF e-statement from your Nigerian corporate bank (Access, Zenith, GTBank, UBA).
2. Compare bank statement closing balances with your General Ledger cash account balance.
3. Identify statutory deductions such as the ₦50 Electronic Money Transfer Levy (EMTL) and SMS charges.
4. Match deposits against customer invoices and payments against approved vendor bills.
5. Post reconciling adjustment journal entries for interest earned and bank charges.
6. Verify that the variance between the bank statement and the general ledger reaches exactly ₦0.00.
```
