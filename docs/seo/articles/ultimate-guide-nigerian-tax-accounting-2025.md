## 1. The State of Business Accounting in Nigeria

Operating a business in Nigeria—whether in Lagos, Abuja, Kano, or Port Harcourt—comes with a unique set of financial and regulatory realities. Unlike Western markets where tax laws change slowly, Nigerian businesses navigate dynamic statutory changes:
- The **Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020**, requiring double-entry balance, internal controls certification (Section 405), and mandatory 6-year books retention (Section 374).
- The **Nigeria Tax Act 2025**, which modernizes Company Income Tax (CIT) brackets and turnover exemption thresholds.
- Overlapping federal and state taxation (FIRS / NRS, State Internal Revenue Services like Lagos LIRS, Rivers RIRS, and local government levies).

To succeed, Nigerian entrepreneurs can no longer rely on paper receipts or basic spreadsheets. A single oversight on a Value Added Tax (VAT) remittance or Withholding Tax (WHT) schedule can lead to debilitating fines and tax audit penalties.

## 2. Understanding 7.5% Value Added Tax (VAT)

### What Is VAT in Nigeria?
Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax charged on the supply of taxable goods and services in Nigeria at a standard statutory rate of **7.5%**.

### The Mathematical Formula:
$$\text{Output VAT} = \text{Net Taxable Amount} \times 0.075$$
$$\text{Total Invoice Amount} = \text{Net Taxable Amount} + \text{Output VAT}$$

For example, if your business sells industrial equipment worth **₦1,000,000.00**:
- Net Sales Revenue: **₦1,000,000.00**
- Output VAT (7.5%): **₦75,000.00**
- Total Billed to Customer: **₦1,075,000.00**

### Statutory Invariant: The GL Account Split
In proper accounting, VAT collected is **not revenue**. It is a current liability held in trust for the Federal Government:
- **Debit `1110 Accounts Receivable`:** ₦1,075,000.00
- **Credit `4010 Operating Revenue`:** ₦1,000,000.00
- **Credit `2010 Output VAT Payable`:** ₦75,000.00

### Monthly Remittance Deadline:
All VAT collected during a calendar month must be remitted to the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) / Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) on or before the **21st day of the following month**.

## 3. Withholding Tax (WHT): The Hidden Asset Most Businesses Lose

One of the most widespread challenges Nigerian businesses face is Withholding Tax (WHT).

When a corporate client pays your invoice, Nigerian tax law requires them to deduct a statutory percentage (usually 2%, 5%, or 10%) at source and remit it directly to the tax authority in your company's name.

### Common Nigerian WHT Statutory Rates:
- **Supply of Goods / Contracts:** `2.0%`
- **Construction & Civil Engineering Works:** `2.5%`
- **Professional, Technical & Consulting Fees:** `5.0%` (Individual) / `10.0%` (Corporate)
- **Commercial Rent & Leases:** `10.0%`

### The Big Trap: Don't Record WHT as an Expense!
Many business owners incorrectly record deducted WHT as a "bank charge" or "loss." **This is a costly mistake!**

Withholding Tax deducted by your client is a **prepaid tax asset** (`GL 1150 WHT Credit Notes Receivable`). When your client remits this deduction, FIRS issues a **WHT Credit Note** with a unique credit reference. You can legally submit these credit notes to offset your annual Company Income Tax (CIT) liability!

## 4. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025: What Small Businesses Must Know

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 introduced monumental reforms to empower small and medium enterprises (SMEs):

### 1. Small Company Exemption (0% CIT)
- Companies with an annual gross turnover of **₦100,000,000.00 or less** are categorized as Small Companies and are subject to **0% Company Income Tax (CIT)**.
- Companies with turnover between **₦100,000,000.00 and ₦250,000,000.00** pay a graduated medium-company rate.
- Companies with turnover exceeding **₦250,000,000.00** pay the standard 30% CIT rate.

### 2. The Critical Professional Services Exclusion!
> **Warning for Consultants, Lawyers, and Accountants:**  
> Under Section 56 of the Tax Act 2025, companies providing **professional, management, medical, engineering, or legal consultancy services** are explicitly **disqualified** from the small company 0% tax exemption, even if their annual turnover is under ₦100,000,000!

## 5. CAMA 2020 Compliance: 6-Year Records & Period Locks

Under the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020:
1. **Section 374 (Mandatory 6-Year Retention):** Every Nigerian registered company must preserve its accounting records, journal vouchers, and financial books for a minimum of **6 years** from the date they are made.
2. **Section 377 (Double-Entry Balance):** All accounting records must be maintained under strict double-entry principles, where debits always equal credits.
3. **Section 405 (Segregation of Duties & Period Certification):** Directors and chief financial officers must certify that internal financial controls have been evaluated. The certifying officer cannot be the same individual who prepared or posted the journal vouchers.

## 6. Mastering Nigerian Bank Statement Reconciliation

Reconciling bank accounts is often the most time-consuming chore in a finance department. Nigerian bank statements feature localized complexities:
- **Electronic Money Transfer Levy (EMTL):** A statutory ₦50 charge deducted on inward bank transfers of ₦10,000 and above.
- **SMS Alert Charges:** ₦4.00 per alert.
- **CBN Foreign Exchange Fluctuations:** Domiciliary account balances requiring revaluation at closing NAFEM rates.
